# Genesis Scene Guide

This guide defines the historical tone, geography, discoveries, and visual language for the 11 Genesis exploration locations. The world is viewed through the explorer's imperfect reconstruction: grounded, reverent, and visibly ancient, with holographic overlays used only for recovered data.

## 1. Garden of Eden — Genesis 1–3

- **Context:** The ordered world, humanity's vocation, temptation, exile, and the first promise of redemption.
- **Geography:** A headwater garden feeding four rivers; its exact earthly location is intentionally unresolved.
- **Atmosphere:** Lush abundance shifts from dawn-bright harmony to wind, shadow, and guarded distance.
- **Discovery:** The explorer reconstructs humanity's calling to bear God's image and records the fracture caused by distrust.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Four branching streams, fruit trees, cultivated paths, paired human silhouettes in hologram, fig leaves, fiery guardian light at the eastern gate.

## 2. East of Eden — Genesis 4

- **Context:** Cain and Abel, the first murder, judgment, wandering, and the beginnings of city life.
- **Geography:** Dry agricultural ground beyond Eden, with pasture meeting tilled fields and a distant early settlement.
- **Atmosphere:** Quiet tension; warm soil tones interrupted by cold evening shadows.
- **Discovery:** A cracked offering stone and field boundary reveal how envy grows when anger is left unmastered.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Furrows, shepherd's cairn, two altars, darkened field, protective mark rendered as an unreadable scan glyph, settlement lights on the horizon.

## 3. Ancient World — Genesis 5

- **Context:** The line from Adam to Noah, long lives, repeated deaths, and Enoch's exceptional walk with God.
- **Geography:** A broad migration corridor layered with generations rather than one fixed city.
- **Atmosphere:** Elegiac and spacious, with dawn-to-night cycles suggesting centuries passing.
- **Discovery:** The ship assembles a luminous genealogy whose repeating pattern is interrupted by hope.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Standing stones, family marks, fading footprints, branching holographic lineage, one trail rising into starlight.

## 4. Mount Ararat — Genesis 6–9

- **Context:** Human violence, Noah's obedience, the flood, preservation, covenant, and the rainbow sign.
- **Geography:** A high mountain ridge above receding waters; avoid claiming a precise archaeological site.
- **Atmosphere:** Storm-black confinement opens into cold blue air, wet stone, and renewed color.
- **Discovery:** Timber fragments and pitch traces point to rescue through judgment and a covenant extended to all living creatures.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Ark silhouette, rain bands, floating debris, mountain snow, raven and dove, olive leaf, altar smoke, rainbow over dark water.

## 5. Plain of Shinar — Genesis 10–11

- **Context:** Nations spread after the flood; Babel concentrates power and reputation before languages are confused.
- **Geography:** Flat alluvial plain between the Tigris and Euphrates, represented without asserting an exact ruin.
- **Atmosphere:** Immense sunset, kiln heat, dust, and an unfinished monument dominating the horizon.
- **Discovery:** A thoroughly burned brick and bitumen residue restore the tower's story and its warning about collective pride.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Stepped tower, brick kilns, canal lines, scaffold silhouettes, scattered tools, glyphs splitting into many scripts, data transmitter beam.

## 6. Land of Canaan — Genesis 12–14

- **Context:** Abram's call, journeys, failures, separation from Lot, rescue, and blessing from Melchizedek.
- **Geography:** Hill country routes between Shechem, Bethel, the Negev, and the Jordan valley.
- **Atmosphere:** Wide horizons and portable camp life; promise is felt more than possessed.
- **Discovery:** Altar sites and caravan traces show faith expressed through movement, worship, and costly rescue.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Tents, oaks, stone altars, star field, ridge paths, distant fertile valley, caravan tracks, bread-and-wine table.

## 7. Oaks of Mamre — Genesis 15–20

- **Context:** Covenant, Hagar and Ishmael, new names, covenant sign, heavenly visitors, intercession, and Sodom's fall.
- **Geography:** Hebron's uplands under great trees, contrasted with the lower Jordan plain.
- **Atmosphere:** Intimate shade and hospitality give way to night sky awe and distant smoke.
- **Discovery:** The explorer recovers covenant markers that hold together promise, patience, justice, and mercy.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Ancient oak canopy, tent entrance, divided offering path, countless stars, three visitors, distant valley glow, salt-white ridge.

## 8. Mount Moriah — Genesis 21–25

- **Context:** Isaac's birth, Hagar's provision, Abraham's test, Sarah's burial, Rebekah's arrival, and Abraham's death.
- **Geography:** Wells and southern camps lead toward a rocky ascent and later back to family burial land.
- **Atmosphere:** Tender family joy mixed with severe testing; the mountain scene resolves in relief rather than spectacle.
- **Discovery:** A ram's horn, well rope, and burial seal connect provision with the continuity of the promise.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Laughter motif, desert well, mother and child silhouettes, mountain path, bound wood, ram in thicket, cave entrance, camel caravan.

## 9. Beer-lahai-roi — Genesis 26–28

- **Context:** Isaac's wells, conflict and peace, Jacob and Esau, the blessing, exile, and the stairway dream.
- **Geography:** Semi-arid wells and grazing land between the Negev and the road north.
- **Atmosphere:** Dusty rivalry becomes a silent, star-filled night of unexpected encounter.
- **Discovery:** Reopened wells and a stone pillow show inherited promise continuing through flawed people.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Well shafts, contested boundary posts, red stew pot, goat-hair disguise clue, lone stone pillow, luminous stairway, ascending messengers.

## 10. Paddan-aram — Genesis 29–36

- **Context:** Jacob's marriages and children, years with Laban, return journey, wrestling, reconciliation, and settlement.
- **Geography:** Northern river country, pasture camps, and the long road back across the Jabbok into Canaan.
- **Atmosphere:** Busy household life and patterned flocks transition into moonlit fear, struggle, and dawn reconciliation.
- **Discovery:** Household seals, breeding rods, and a boundary heap trace transformation from grasping to humbled perseverance.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Village well, veiled wedding tent, spotted flocks, striped rods, household idols, stone witness heap, river ford, wrestling silhouettes, sunrise embrace.

## 11. Egypt — Genesis 37–50

- **Context:** Joseph is sold, suffers, interprets dreams, rises to administer Egypt, preserves life through famine, reconciles with family, and dies in hope.
- **Geography:** Canaan cistern and caravan road lead to Nile store cities, prison chambers, palace courts, and Goshen.
- **Atmosphere:** Saturated Egyptian color and monumental scale, balanced by small human spaces of grief, integrity, and forgiveness.
- **Discovery:** A torn robe fiber, seal impression, grain token, and embalmed coffin reveal providence working through betrayal and displacement.
- **Pixel-art anchors:** Multicolored garment, dry cistern, merchant caravan, prison bars, paired dream symbols, signet ring, granaries, famine queues, family wagons, blessing tableau, coffin facing the road home.

## Shared Art Direction

- Compose every scene on the 192×108 logical canvas and scale with nearest-neighbor rendering.
- Keep readable silhouettes and no more than three dominant depth planes.
- Use warm sand and fire colors for the ancient world; reserve cyan for the explorer, ship, scans, and recovered holograms.
- Avoid depicting God directly. Use environment, light, sound, text, and character response.
- Treat uncertain locations and dates honestly in dialogue and journal text.
- Build discoveries around a place clue, a Scripture clue, and a material relic so scenes can share one data-driven progression pattern.
